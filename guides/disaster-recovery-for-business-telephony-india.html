Disaster Recovery for Business Telephony: An India-Focused Checklist

Keep customer calls working when an office, ISP, device, route or telephony platform fails.

India-focusedPractical planning guideReviewed 10 Sep 2026

Quick answer

Telephony disaster recovery needs more than a backup internet link. Decide where inbound numbers reroute, how agents log in from another site, how CRM and recordings continue, how outbound caller identity works and who declares failover. Test the whole path at least a few times a year.

A company can have a secondary ISP and still lose calling because DNS, firewall policy, SIP routing, softphones or power were never tested on the backup path. Recovery is an end-to-end workflow.

This guide uses simple planning logic rather than pretending there is one universal number for every business. Start with your own traffic data whenever you have it. If you do not, use conservative assumptions and replace them once real calls start flowing.

Worked example

Put the numbers into a real scenario

A two-site support team in Gurugram and Pune can configure the main number to overflow to the second site if the first queue is unreachable. Agents should have laptops and softphones that can work from a backup internet connection. Quarterly tests should record the actual recovery time.

India-specific checks

Validate the plan with current sources and provider documentation

When you compare a real implementation, review more than feature names. For example, Acefone publishes information on cloud contact center and cloud telephony services. You can compare the same requirement against ZPE network resilience blueprint so you can see how another provider or technical source describes the workflow, packaging or architecture. For another commercial comparison, you can also review Exotel business phone system pricing. For standards or regulatory context, use IETF RFC 3550 for RTP. Treat provider pages as product information and the official source as the authority when the two cover a regulated or standards-based point.

For commercial calling, do not assume that a technically possible call flow is automatically compliant. Number-series rules, customer preference, consent, auto-dialer use and other requirements can change. Check current official guidance and ask the selected telecom provider to explain how your exact flow is implemented in India.

Questions to put in your provider requirement sheet

  • What exact limit applies to agents, simultaneous calls, numbers and monthly usage?
  • How quickly can capacity be increased during a campaign or unexpected surge?
  • What is included in the quoted price and what is billed separately?
  • What happens to inbound routing, recordings and CRM logging during an internet or platform failure?
  • Which current TRAI, DoT or platform requirements apply to this exact use case, and how are they enforced in the product?

Practical planning

Build telephony recovery around dependencies, not only the voice platform

A cloud phone service can be healthy while your business is still unable to take calls because an office internet link, CRM, identity system, power supply or carrier route has failed. A useful disaster recovery plan maps these dependencies and gives each critical call flow a tested fallback.

List the failures that can stop a customer call

Start with inbound number routing, internet access, agent devices, authentication, CRM, power and the provider itself. For each item, write the customer impact and the temporary workaround. A simple table often reveals that the recovery plan only covers one type of outage.

Prioritise the numbers and queues that matter most. A revenue line, emergency support queue and internal extension do not need the same recovery target.

Make alternate routing easy to activate

Decide in advance where calls should go if the normal queue is unavailable. That may be another office, a mobile team, a backup number or a recorded message with a callback process. Keep the routing change documented and restrict who can activate it.

If staff may work from another location during an incident, confirm that devices, VPN rules, authentication and internet access are ready there. Recovery is not complete when the number rings if the agent cannot see the customer record or complete the task.

Run short recovery drills

Test one scenario at a time, such as an office internet outage or unavailable CRM. Measure how long it takes to detect the issue, change routing and restore normal service. Record what was confusing and update the runbook.

Keep provider escalation contacts and account identifiers accessible outside the failed system. A recovery document stored only inside an unavailable corporate tool is not useful during an incident.

Use this quick check before you finalise the plan

  • Map number, network, identity, CRM and power dependencies.
  • Rank queues by business criticality.
  • Document alternate routing for important numbers.
  • Keep recovery access outside the primary failed system.
  • Test agent operation from the backup location.
  • Run and record small recovery drills several times a year.

FAQ

Common questions

Is the disaster recovery for business telephony calculation exact?

No. CallSizer gives a planning estimate. Real capacity depends on provider architecture, actual traffic patterns, call flow, network behaviour and commercial terms. Use the result to build a requirement sheet, then validate it with a pilot.

Should I use daily averages for planning?

Use daily averages for budgeting, but not for peak capacity. Busy-hour or 30-minute interval data is much safer for staffing, concurrency and queue decisions.

Should I choose a provider before doing the calculation?

Usually no. First estimate your requirement. Then every provider can quote against the same number of agents, channels, minutes, integrations and features.

What should an Indian business verify before going live?

Verify number provisioning, support process, data and recording requirements, network readiness, failover and the current TRAI or DoT rules that apply to your calling use case. The exact compliance requirement can differ between service, transactional and promotional communication.
