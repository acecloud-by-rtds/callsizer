Cloud Contact Center Migration Checklist for Indian Teams

Move from an old PBX or call-center platform without losing numbers, routing, recordings or reporting.

India-focusedPractical planning guideReviewed 10 Sep 2026

Quick answer

Treat contact-center migration as a call-flow and data migration, not just a software change. Inventory numbers, IVRs, queues, agent groups, recordings, integrations, reports, compliance controls and failover. Test them in parallel before the final cutover.

A migration can look successful because agents can log in and make calls, while important behaviour is still missing. Common gaps include old numbers, after-hours routing, CRM screen pops, recording retrieval, supervisor functions and campaign rules.

This guide uses simple planning logic rather than pretending there is one universal number for every business. Start with your own traffic data whenever you have it. If you do not, use conservative assumptions and replace them once real calls start flowing.

Worked example

Put the numbers into a real scenario

A 60-agent support center may pilot five agents and one low-volume number for a week. Once CRM logging, recordings and reports match expectations, move the next queue. The final high-volume number should switch only after rollback routing has been tested.

Questions to put in your provider requirement sheet

  • What exact limit applies to agents, simultaneous calls, numbers and monthly usage?
  • How quickly can capacity be increased during a campaign or unexpected surge?
  • What is included in the quoted price and what is billed separately?
  • What happens to inbound routing, recordings and CRM logging during an internet or platform failure?
  • Which current TRAI, DoT or platform requirements apply to this exact use case, and how are they enforced in the product?

Practical planning

Reduce migration risk by moving call flows in controlled stages

A contact center migration touches numbers, routing, agents, reporting, integrations, recordings and business continuity at the same time. The safest plan is to inventory the current environment, build the new one in parallel, test real workflows and move traffic in stages with a documented rollback path.

Inventory what exists before redesigning it

List public numbers, IVRs, queues, business hours, agent groups, recording rules, integrations, reports and special routing exceptions. Identify which items are still needed. Migration is a good time to remove old queues and undocumented workarounds rather than recreating them in a new platform.

Capture baseline metrics such as call volume, peak concurrency, AHT, abandonment and transfer patterns. These numbers help size the new system and give you something to compare after cutover.

Test integrations and identity early

CRM, SSO, workforce tools and reporting exports often create more migration work than basic calling. Test them before training the full agent population. Include failure cases such as a CRM timeout, expired user session or missing customer record. When you turn this step into a vendor requirement, review contact center providers to see the controls a real platform exposes, then compare those controls against your own workflow.

Create users and permissions with a repeatable process. A cloud platform makes it easy to add seats, but inconsistent roles and supervisor access can become an operational problem if hundreds of accounts are created manually.

Use a pilot queue and a rollback trigger

Move one team, location or low-risk number first. Keep the old environment available long enough to compare call quality, routing, reports and agent workflow. Define the conditions that would make you pause or roll back, such as failed inbound routing or missing CRM logs.

After the pilot is stable, migrate in waves. Update runbooks and training material with what the pilot taught you instead of assuming the original plan was complete.

Use this quick check before you finalise the plan

  • Create an inventory of numbers, queues and integrations.
  • Measure current peak traffic before sizing the new system.
  • Test CRM, SSO and reporting before mass user rollout.
  • Pilot one queue or location first.
  • Define rollback triggers and owners.
  • Reconcile call reports before switching off the old platform.

FAQ

Common questions

Is the cloud contact center migration checklist for indian teams calculation exact?

No. CallSizer gives a planning estimate. Real capacity depends on provider architecture, actual traffic patterns, call flow, network behaviour and commercial terms. Use the result to build a requirement sheet, then validate it with a pilot.

Should I use daily averages for planning?

Use daily averages for budgeting, but not for peak capacity. Busy-hour or 30-minute interval data is much safer for staffing, concurrency and queue decisions.

Should I choose a provider before doing the calculation?

Usually no. First estimate your requirement. Then every provider can quote against the same number of agents, channels, minutes, integrations and features.

What should an Indian business verify before going live?

Verify number provisioning, support process, data and recording requirements, network readiness, failover and the current TRAI or DoT rules that apply to your calling use case. The exact compliance requirement can differ between service, transactional and promotional communication.
