How Much VoIP Bandwidth Do 20, 50 or 100 Agents Need?

Plan voice bandwidth using concurrent calls, codec rate, packet overhead and safety margin.

India-focusedPractical planning guideReviewed 10 Sep 2026

Quick answer

Size VoIP bandwidth from concurrent calls, not total employees. A codec payload rate is only part of the traffic because RTP, UDP and IP headers add overhead. Calculate both upload and download requirements and keep spare capacity for CRM, browsers, screen sharing and other office traffic.

Teams often buy an internet connection by looking only at the codec label. That can understate real usage. A 64 kbps G.711 audio payload, for example, creates a higher on-wire rate after packet headers and packetisation are included.

This guide uses simple planning logic rather than pretending there is one universal number for every business. Start with your own traffic data whenever you have it. If you do not, use conservative assumptions and replace them once real calls start flowing.

Step by step

A practical way to plan it

1

Estimate simultaneous calls

Use the number of calls that can be active at once. Fifty agents do not automatically mean fifty calls. If your peak is 22 calls, use 22 as the voice-concurrency base.

2

Choose the codec assumption

Ask your provider which codec and packetisation interval are used. G.711 prioritises voice quality but uses more bandwidth. Compressed codecs reduce payload size but can add processing and quality trade-offs.

3

Include protocol overhead

RTP commonly carries real-time audio and is usually transported over UDP/IP. The IETF notes that network and transport overhead matters when estimating session bandwidth, so do not multiply codec payload alone.

4

Add headroom and separate traffic

Keep 25% to 40% voice headroom, then make sure the WAN still has space for web apps, CRM, file sync and video. Consider QoS so bursty office traffic does not crowd out voice packets.

Worked example

Put the numbers into a real scenario

A 50-agent team may have 25 concurrent calls. If your planning assumption is about 90 kbps in each direction per G.711 call after common IP overhead, voice alone is roughly 2.25 Mbps up and 2.25 Mbps down. With 35% headroom, plan for about 3.1 Mbps each way just for voice, then add normal business traffic.

India-specific checks

Validate the plan with current sources and provider documentation

When you compare a real implementation, review more than feature names. For example, Acefone publishes information on cloud telephony services and contact center service. You can compare the same requirement against Tata Tele Business Services so you can see how another provider or technical source describes the workflow, packaging or architecture. For another commercial comparison, you can also review Ozonetel cloud contact center. For standards or regulatory context, use IETF RFC 3550 for RTP. Treat provider pages as product information and the official source as the authority when the two cover a regulated or standards-based point.

For commercial calling, do not assume that a technically possible call flow is automatically compliant. Number-series rules, customer preference, consent, auto-dialer use and other requirements can change. Check current official guidance and ask the selected telecom provider to explain how your exact flow is implemented in India.

Questions to put in your provider requirement sheet

  • What exact limit applies to agents, simultaneous calls, numbers and monthly usage?
  • How quickly can capacity be increased during a campaign or unexpected surge?
  • What is included in the quoted price and what is billed separately?
  • What happens to inbound routing, recordings and CRM logging during an internet or platform failure?
  • Which current TRAI, DoT or platform requirements apply to this exact use case, and how are they enforced in the product?

Practical planning

Calculate bandwidth from simultaneous calls, then test the real network

Headcount is only an indirect input for VoIP bandwidth. Twenty agents do not always mean twenty active calls, and one hundred agents may have very different concurrency depending on the operation. Estimate simultaneous calls first, multiply by the per-call network requirement for the chosen codec, then add headroom and test under normal office traffic.

Use concurrency instead of logged-in seats

Start with the busiest expected number of simultaneous conversations. An inbound support team may have high concurrency, while an outbound team with low connect rates can have many agents logged in but fewer active calls. Use real call records when available.

If your provider uses browser calling, softphones or mobile apps, confirm the codec and network guidance for that client. The same organisation can have different bandwidth behaviour across endpoints.

Remember protocol overhead and both directions

The audio codec bitrate is not the entire packet size. Real-time voice also carries protocol headers, and the network needs reliable upload as well as download capacity. Use the provider's documented figures or a measured sample rather than budgeting only for the nominal codec rate.

If calls are recorded in the cloud, the office may not upload a separate recording stream, but screen sharing, CRM traffic and other tools still use the same connection. Keep non-voice traffic in the network plan.

Test while the office is busy

Run calls while video meetings, uploads and normal work are happening. Watch for jitter, latency and packet loss rather than only total throughput. A connection can have high advertised speed and still deliver unstable voice when queues form in the router or ISP path.

Recheck after office growth or a network change. Adding cameras, backup software or another floor can change conditions even if the telephony configuration stays the same.

Use this quick check before you finalise the plan

  • Estimate simultaneous calls before bandwidth.
  • Use current provider codec guidance.
  • Budget upload and download capacity.
  • Leave headroom for normal business traffic.
  • Test jitter and packet loss during busy periods.
  • Reassess after network, office or headcount changes.

FAQ

Common questions

Is the how much voip bandwidth do 20, 50 or 100 agents need? calculation exact?

No. CallSizer gives a planning estimate. Real capacity depends on provider architecture, actual traffic patterns, call flow, network behaviour and commercial terms. Use the result to build a requirement sheet, then validate it with a pilot.

Should I use daily averages for planning?

Use daily averages for budgeting, but not for peak capacity. Busy-hour or 30-minute interval data is much safer for staffing, concurrency and queue decisions.

Should I choose a provider before doing the calculation?

Usually no. First estimate your requirement. Then every provider can quote against the same number of agents, channels, minutes, integrations and features.

What should an Indian business verify before going live?

Verify number provisioning, support process, data and recording requirements, network readiness, failover and the current TRAI or DoT rules that apply to your calling use case. The exact compliance requirement can differ between service, transactional and promotional communication.
